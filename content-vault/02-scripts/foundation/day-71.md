## AM — LEARN — Price the first version intelligently

**Full script (25–40 sec):**
> [CAREN: name the venture and the price you almost set too low, and why]
> My first instinct was to price it low so people couldn't say no. That's
> backwards — a price that's too low signals uncertainty, and it makes
> every future price increase feel like a betrayal instead of a natural
> step. [CAREN: show the actual number you landed on instead, and the
> reasoning] Price for what it's actually worth to the person paying, not
> for what feels safe to charge.

## MIDDAY — DO — Price the first version intelligently

**Full script (25–40 sec):**
> Assignment: write down what you're about to charge, then write one
> sentence describing the value the customer gets — the time saved, the
> outcome, the relief. [CAREN: do this live for one venture's pricing] If
> the number doesn't feel proportional to that sentence, that's your sign to
> adjust — probably up, not down. This is general pricing philosophy, not
> advice for your specific market. Save this, write both lines, and compare
> them honestly.

*EXAMPLE FILL — swap for your real week:* writing "saves 6 hours of manual
work a week" next to the public-adjusting business's proposed service fee

## PM — CONNECT — Price the first version intelligently

**Full script (25–40 sec):**
> Honestly: [CAREN: what was the actual fear behind pricing low — fear of
> rejection, fear of being seen as too ambitious, something else?] Pricing
> low wasn't really about the customer. It was about not wanting to hear
> "no" to a number I'd have to stand behind. A low price is easy to defend
> because it never really gets tested. Beyond the Pitch means setting the
> real number and finding out, on camera, whether I was right to believe in
> it. If pricing scares you more than building does, save this.
