## MIDDAY — DO — Name your business strategically

**Full script (25–40 sec):**
> Assignment: take your current name (or top candidate) and run it through
> three tests. Say it out loud to someone without spelling it — can they
> spell it back? Say what the business does in one sentence using the name
> — does it still make sense in five years? [CAREN: run this test live on
> a real name from the portfolio] If it fails two of three, that's useful
> information, not a crisis. Save this, run the test, and note what you
> find.

*EXAMPLE FILL — swap for your real week:* running the spell-back test on a
name candidate with a friend over the phone and watching it fail on the
first try.

**On-screen text:**
```
DO THIS TODAY
3 tests: say it, spell it, stretch it 5 years.
```

**Storyboard:**
1. **(0–3s) Hook** — Text card: "Your 10-minute assignment" over a phone call setup.
2. **(3–10s) Struggle** — Saying the name into the phone, waiting for the spell-back.
3. **(10–24s) Shift** — Writing down the result — pass or fail — for each test.
4. **(24–32s) Proof** — Final tally: how many tests it passed.
5. **(32–40s) Close** — End card: "Save this. Do it today."

**AI image prompts:**
1. "Close-up of a woman on a phone call, mid-conversation, warm natural light, editorial photography, candid, Beyond the Pitch palette."
2. "Overhead shot of a notebook with three test criteria written down and checkmarks or X's beside each, warm natural light, clean composition."
3. "Close-up of a hand writing a final tally at the bottom of a notebook page, terracotta ink, warm window light."

**Photo-to-video motion:** Standard day spec; no unusual variation, procedural pacing matching the three-test structure.

**B-roll:** Phone resting after the call; hand marking a checklist.

**Caption:**

## PM — CONNECT — Name your business strategically

**Full script (25–40 sec):**
> Honest admission: [CAREN: how much time did the naming process actually
> cost you, and what did you tell yourself while you were stuck on it?] I
> told myself the name mattered more than it did, because a name is a
> decision I could make alone in a notebook — unlike almost everything else
> in the business. Sometimes the "easy" decision becomes the one you hide in
> longest. Beyond the Pitch is naming that pattern out loud, not just the
> decisions themselves. If you've ever stalled on something small to avoid
> something big, save this.

*EXAMPLE FILL — swap for your real week:* three days spent on Lumina's name
